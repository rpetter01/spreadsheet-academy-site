SUMIF and COUNTIF in Excel: Add and Count With a Condition

Published November 18, 2026 · 5 min read

SUM adds everything and COUNT counts everything. SUMIF and COUNTIF do the same, but only for the rows that match a condition: the total for one region, the number of sales above a value. Here is how to use them.

The sample table

These sales are in A1:C6:

ABC
1RegionSellerSales
2NorthAna1200
3SouthBen800
4NorthCy1500
5EastDee900
6SouthEli1100

SUMIF: add up one group

=SUMIF(range, criteria, [sum_range])
  • range is where Excel checks the condition.
  • criteria is the condition.
  • sum_range is the column to add up.

The total sales of the North region:

=SUMIF(A2:A6,"North",C2:C6)

Excel looks at A2:A6, finds the rows that say North (rows 2 and 4), and adds the matching values in column C: 1200 + 1500 = 2700.

COUNTIF: count one group

=COUNTIF(range, criteria)

How many sales came from the North?

=COUNTIF(A2:A6,"North")

The answer is 2.

Conditions with comparison signs

The criteria can also be a comparison. The sign goes inside the quotes, together with the number:

=COUNTIF(C2:C6,">1000")

This counts the sales above 1000: 1200, 1500 and 1100, so the answer is 3. To add them up instead, the range and the sum range are the same column, so you can leave the third part out:

=SUMIF(C2:C6,">1000")

The answer is 1200 + 1500 + 1100 = 3800.

Use a cell as the condition

If you type the region in E2, you can point to it, and the formula works for any region you type:

=SUMIF(A2:A6,E2,C2:C6)

To combine a sign with a cell, join them with &: ">"&E2.

More than one condition: SUMIFS and COUNTIFS

When you need two or more conditions at once, add an S. In SUMIFS the column to add up comes first:

=SUMIFS(C2:C6,A2:A6,"North",C2:C6,">1200")

Only the North sales above 1200 count, which leaves Cy's 1500, so the answer is 1500.

Common mistakes

  • Ranges of different sizes. The range and the sum range must have the same number of rows.
  • A sign without quotes. Write ">1000", not >1000.
  • Mixing the order in SUMIFS. The column to add up always comes first there, unlike in SUMIF.
